Where the fair line comes from

Every two-sided price includes the book's cut. Pinnacle takes bets from sharp players with high limits, so its line is the best public estimate of the true odds. The scanner removes the vig from the sharp book's market (a devig) to get each side's true win probability.

Pinnacle at +139 / −154 implies 41.8% + 60.6% = 102.4%. The power method shrinks both sides until they sum to 100%, giving the Lakers a 40.5% fair chance, or +147.

When the sharp book has no line, the scanner averages the devigged prices of every other book with a full market (at least three), leaving out the book being priced.

Edge, stake and arbitrage

edge = fair win % × decimal odds − 1

FanDuel's Lakers +160 pays 2.60 back per dollar, so 0.405 × 2.60 − 1 = +5.3%. Over many bets that size of edge returns about 5 cents per dollar staked.

The stake column uses the Kelly criterion, edge ÷ (decimal odds − 1), scaled down by your Kelly fraction. Quarter Kelly is the common choice because the fair line itself is an estimate.

An arbitrage is a set of prices at different books whose implied chances add up to under 100%. Splitting the stake in proportion to 1 ÷ odds returns the same amount whichever side wins.

Run live scans

The page opens on a sample slate with invented prices. To scan live odds:

  1. Get a free key at the-odds-api.com. The free plan has 500 credits a month.
  2. Paste it under Odds data in the settings. It stays in your browser and is only sent to The Odds API.
  3. Pick your sports, markets and books, then press Scan books.

Running your own copy? Serve the folder over http rather than opening the file directly, because browsers block API calls from files on disk:

python3 -m http.server 8000

Each scan costs one credit per sport per market when you pick ten books or fewer. Four sports with all three markets is 12 credits.

Checking that the fair line works

Log the bets you place. The Bet log compares each price you took with the fair price right before the game started. That gap is closing line value. Bettors who consistently beat the closing line win over time, and it shows after 50 to 100 bets, long before your win/loss record means anything.

The log also compares your actual profit with what the fair line expected. If you beat the close but profit lags, that is usually ordinary variance. If you don't beat the close, the edges are disappearing before you bet.

Before you bet

  • Edges close quickly. Confirm the price at the book before you place it.
  • Edges over 12% get a Check line tag. They usually mean a stale price, a different line, or a market the feed mislabeled.
  • Pinnacle prices in this feed come from its public site and can lag by a minute or two.
  • A positive-EV bet still loses often. A 5% edge on a +160 underdog loses about 60% of the time; the profit only shows across hundreds of bets.
  • US books limit accounts that consistently beat the closing line.
